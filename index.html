About

I study how large structural variants reshape genomes and drive evolution, combining population genomics with wet lab molecular biology.

I am a genomics researcher with 8 years of wet lab and 6 years of computational experience studying structural variation and its role in evolution. My work in the McGrath Lab at Georgia Tech combines Bionano optical genome mapping, PacBio HiFi sequencing, and Illumina short-read analysis to understand how large chromosomal inversions shape the adaptive radiation of Lake Malawi cichlid fishes.

I am driven by the challenge of connecting bench-level experimental design with computational pipelines that extract biological insight from complex genomic datasets. I defended my dissertation in May 2026.

Research

PhD Thesis

Structural Variation in Adaptive Radiation: Determining the Potential Role of Large Genomic Inversions in Lake Malawi Cichlids

Lake Malawi is home to one of the most dramatic vertebrate radiations on Earth: over 800 cichlid species evolved in about 1.2 million years. Large chromosomal inversions are consequential in this process, since suppressed recombination between inverted and non-inverted regions locks co-adaptive alleles into a single inherited block. I identified **six large inversions** spanning over 12% of the cichlid genome and demonstrated their roles in habitat adaptation, lineage divergence, and sex determination.

I discovered these inversions with optical genome mapping and validated them with 6 de novo PacBio HiFi assemblies. Short-read genotyping across **118 species** from all seven lake ecogroups showed that inversion genotype correlates with habitat preference, and that three inversions likely spread between lineages via ancient hybridization.

One of these inversions also determines sex. In a deep benthic *Aulonocara* species, the chromosome 10 inversion functions as an XY sex-determination system , with males heterozygous and females homozygous. Backcrossing to a species lacking the inversion restored recombination across the male-specific Y, and QTL mapping followed by fine-mapping narrowed the locus to a 200 kb window containing a long non-coding RNA that carries transposable element derived indels found only on the putative Y.

Previous Work

HIV Reservoir Characterization — CFAR, UC San Diego

Studied the HIV reservoir in terminal patient tissues from people living with HIV under long-term antiretroviral therapy. Used droplet digital PCR and sequence alignment to quantify suppressed viral load and characterize reservoir diversity across tissues.
