About

I am a genomics researcher with 8 years of wet lab and 6 years of computational experience studying structural variation and its role in evolution. My PhD work in the McGrath Lab at Georgia Tech combines Bionano optical genome mapping, PacBio HiFi sequencing, and Illumina short-read analysis to understand how large chromosomal inversions shape the adaptive radiation of Lake Malawi cichlid fishes.

I am driven by the challenge of connecting bench-level experimental design with computational pipelines that extract biological insight from complex genomic datasets. I defend my dissertation in May 2026.

Research

PhD Thesis

Structural Variation in Adaptive Radiation: Large Genomic Inversions in Lake Malawi Cichlids

Lake Malawi is home to over 800 species of cichlid fishes that evolved in approximately 1.2 million years—one of the most dramatic vertebrate radiations on Earth. My work identified six large chromosomal inversions (9.9–22.9 Mb) segregating across the lake's seven major ecogroups and demonstrated their roles in habitat adaptation, lineage divergence, and sex determination.

Using Bionano optical mapping on 31 individuals from 11 species, I discovered these inversions and validated them with 6 de novo PacBio HiFi genome assemblies. I then genotyped 365 individuals using a PCA-based approach from short-read SNV data, revealing that three inversions likely spread between lineages via ancient hybridization. Critically, I showed the chromosome 10 inversion acts as an XY sex-determination system in a deep benthic species.

Previous Work

HIV Reservoir Characterization — CFAR, UC San Diego

Studied the HIV reservoir in terminal patient tissues from people living with HIV under long-term antiretroviral therapy. Used droplet digital PCR and sequence alignment to quantify suppressed viral load and characterize reservoir diversity across tissue compartments.
